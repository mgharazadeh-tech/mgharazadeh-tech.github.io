I am a mechanical engineer with 10 years of experience in static equipment for refineries, gas condensate and petrochemical plants. I also design ASME VIII pressure vessels, such as air receivers and horizontal process vessels, and TEMA U-tube heat exchangers.

I take a job from the first mechanical calculation to approved fabrication drawings and into the shop. I plan plates to save material and keep the design easy to build. I work closely with the piping, civil, process and safety teams, and clients usually approved my drawings at revision 2 or 3.

I am now finishing an M.Sc. in Advanced Manufacturing at TU Chemnitz (February 2027). There I use FEA in Abaqus/Explicit with Python scripts for contact models and polymer cutting. In one study my pulley design lowered the peak contact pressure by 78 %. My aim is a design role for equipment and machines, where I use this simulation and scripting work to check and improve my own designs, not only by code formulas.

P-01

API 650 storage tanks, from data sheet to final documents

I design and draw all common types of API 650 storage tanks, from 6 m to 94 m in diameter, for crude oil, condensate, naphtha, kerosene, glycol, methanol and water. I take each tank from the process data sheet to the full document package for fabrication and erection.

  • Fixed cone roof: self-supported, or on rafters and columns.
  • Dome roof: self-supported, or externally supported on rafters and girders without inner columns.
  • External floating roof: double-deck and pontoon, with rolling ladder, seals, drains and vents.
  • Internal floating roof: under a fixed cone or dome roof.
  • Design for wind, seismic (API 650 Annex E), internal pressure and vacuum (Annex F and V).
What I deliver for each tank
  • Mechanical data sheet and specification
  • Shell, bottom and roof calculation
  • Roof structure and floating roof calculation
  • Wind, seismic and anchor calculation
  • Venting calculation (API 2000)
  • Nozzle and clip local stress calculation
  • General arrangement and orientation
  • Shell, bottom, annular and roof plate drawings
  • Plate cutting plans with minimum waste
  • Nozzles, manholes, sumps, vents, drains
  • Spiral stair, platforms, walkways, handrails
  • Wind girders, cooling water and foam system
  • Material take-off and material requisition
  • ITP, WPS / PQR, NDT, painting, erection and hydrotest procedures

I also coordinate with the piping, civil, process and safety teams on nozzle orientation, foundation loads, tank size and fire protection.

R-01

Less contact pressure between a braided rope and its pulley

Synthetic ropes wear where they press hardest on the pulley. I built a 12-strand braided rope with Python in Grasshopper, modelled four pulley surfaces and compared the peak contact pressure in Abaqus/Explicit. A wavy surface that follows the braid at 45° gave the lowest pressure.

Peak contact pressure, CPRESS (MPa)
Flat (reference)4.519
Grooved2.519
Wavy 30°4.810
Wavy 45°, matched0.980
Solver
Abaqus/Explicit, C3D8R, general contact
Rope
6.5 mm, 12 strands, 40 mm pitch
Pulley
Steel, Johnson–Cook plasticity
Result
78 % lower peak pressure than flat
R-02

Master's thesis: burrs in deformational cutting of PA6

When polyamide 6 is cut by deformation, burrs form and then spring back as the material recovers and gets soft from heat. Current Abaqus models do not show this well. My thesis models how heating or cooling the tool changes the burr.

  • Abaqus/Explicit with Lagrangian and Coupled Eulerian–Lagrangian (CEL) models.
  • Improving the PA6 VUMAT user material for damage and viscoplastic recovery.
  • Python scripts that read residual stress and temperature from the results.
