P-01

API 650 storage tanks, from data sheet to final documents

I design and draw all common types of API 650 storage tanks, from 6 m to 94 m in diameter, for crude oil, condensate, naphtha, kerosene, glycol, methanol and water. I take each tank from the process data sheet to the full document package for fabrication and erection.

  • Fixed cone roof: self-supported, or on rafters and columns.
  • Dome roof: self-supported, or externally supported on rafters and girders without inner columns.
  • External floating roof: double-deck and pontoon, with rolling ladder, seals, drains and vents.
  • Internal floating roof: under a fixed cone or dome roof.
  • Design for wind, seismic (API 650 Annex E), internal pressure and vacuum (Annex F and V).
What I deliver for each tank
  • Calculations and specifications: mechanical data sheet, API 650 and API 2000 calculations (shell, bottom, roof and roof structure, floating roof, wind, seismic, anchors, venting) and nozzle local stress check.
  • Fabrication drawings: general arrangement and orientation, shell, bottom and roof plates with cutting plans for minimum waste, nozzles and manholes, stairs, platforms, wind girders, cooling water and foam system.
  • Procurement and QC documents: material take-off, material requisition, ITP, WPS / PQR, and NDT, painting, erection and hydrotest procedures.

I also coordinate with the piping, civil, process and safety teams on nozzle orientation, foundation loads, tank size and fire protection.

R-01

FEA contact study: braided rope and pulley

Synthetic ropes wear where they press hardest on the pulley. I studied this by comparing the peak contact pressure on different pulley surfaces.

  • Parametric model: a Python script in Rhino/Grasshopper that builds the 3D geometry of a 12-strand braided rope.
  • FEA: a contact model in Abaqus/Explicit to compare four pulley surface designs.
  • Result: a wavy pulley surface that follows the braid at 45° gave the lowest pressure, 78 % lower than a flat pulley.

R-02

Master's thesis: thermo-mechanical modelling of burr formation in PA6

When polyamide 6 (PA6) is cut by deformation, burrs form, spring back and get soft from heat. Standard FEA models do not show this well. My thesis simulates how the temperature of the cutting tool changes burr formation and the recovery of the material.

  • FEA: Abaqus/Explicit with Lagrangian and Coupled Eulerian–Lagrangian (CEL) models for large deformation and material flow.
  • Material model: extending a VUMAT user subroutine for damage, thermal softening and viscoplastic recovery of the polymer.
  • Post-processing: Python scripts that read residual stress and temperature fields from the results.
